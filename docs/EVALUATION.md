# Evaluation methodology and caveats

## Data
- `data/corpus`: 9 synthetic documents (53 chunks), written with deliberately confusable concepts
  (rotation vs retention, rollback vs failover, severity levels, freeze windows).
- `data/eval/golden.jsonl`: 46 questions — 10 lexical, 22 paraphrase, 2 multi-section, 12
  unanswerable. Each has `split` (dev/test) and relevance as `{doc_id, section_contains}`, so labels
  survive changes to the chunker.

## Metrics
Recall@{1,3,5} over required references, MRR@10, nDCG@10 (binary), latency p50/p95. Ablation across
`bm25 / dense / hybrid / hybrid_rerank`. Answer metrics: keyword coverage, citation precision,
false abstention (answerable but refused), correct abstention (unanswerable and refused). A
threshold sweep is reported per split; the recommended `min_support` is chosen **on dev only**.

## Results
See [eval-report.md](eval-report.md) (generated by `docqa eval`).

## What these results do and do not show
1. **One author.** I wrote the corpus and the questions; paraphrases share my vocabulary habits.
2. **Tiny n.** 34 answerable questions: one question is ~3 points of recall. Mode differences are
   noise-level.
3. **LSA ≈ TF-IDF at this size**, hence dense == BM25.
4. **Reranker: no gain**, reported as is.
5. **Failures q29 and q34** are vocabulary mismatches ("reverted without human action" vs the
   document's wording; a multi-section answer). Not patched, to avoid overfitting.
6. **Abstention does not generalise.** Dev picked 0.5 (8% false abstention); test shows 41%.
   I also compared several candidate signals (coverage, IDF-weighted coverage, dense cosine,
   hit count) by AUC in a scratch analysis; none was strong on test (best ≈ 0.81), so I kept the
   simple coverage signal rather than shopping for a feature on 12 test items.
7. **Test split is not pristine**: I had seen aggregate results before splitting.
8. **Not tested live**: Anthropic generator and sentence-transformers adapter.

## Next steps to make this credible
A larger public corpus, independently written questions, a real embedding model, a cross-encoder
reranker, and a groundedness judge for abstention, evaluated with confidence intervals.
